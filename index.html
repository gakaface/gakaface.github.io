Two Types of Bijections and Cantor's Logical Fallacy
Qi Jin  |  2026  |  Zenodo  |  DOI  |  Preprint
There are two types of bijections between infinite sets: partial field bijection and full field bijection. The bijection used to compare the cardinalities of sets are partial field in nature, exemplified by the bijection between even numbers and natural numbers. Cantor failed to distinguish between these two different types of bijections, which led to logical fallacies and erroneous conclusions, such as |N| < |P(N)| and |R| = |P(N)|. This paper logically demonstrates Cantor's logical errors.
Proof of the Continuum Hypothesis
Qi Jin  |  2026  |  Zenodo  |  DOI  |  Preprint
German mathematician Cantor proposed the concept of equipotence for two infinite sets: if a one-to-one mapping can be constructed between the elements of two infinite sets, they are called equipotent. Why can one-to-one mapping distinguish the power of sets? This article provides a clear explanation using computational methods. There are two types of one-to-one mapping: partial field bijective mapping and full field bijective mapping. If these two mappings are not distinguished, it is easy to lead to fallacies when using the method of proof by contradiction to prove non-equipotence. It has been proven through computational methods that Real numbers and natural numbers are not equipotent. This also yields a method for proving irrationality. Finally, the continuum hypothesis was discussed.
On the Proof of the Fifth Postulate (the Parallel Axiom) in Euclidean Geometry
Qi Jin  |  2026  |  Zenodo  |  DOI  |  Preprint
The framework of Euclidean geometry lacks a definitive criterion to distinguish straight lines from curves, prompting the introduction of the Fifth Postulate (the Parallel Axiom) as a logical patch. However, this postulate can be proven without new axioms by utilizing the concept of area. The core distinction lies in the fact that equal line segments can coincide after translation on a plane, but not necessarily on curved surfaces. This translational property enables the proof of the Parallel Postulate on a plane without contradicting the existence of non-Euclidean geometries.
Proof of the Transcendence of the Euler–Mascheroni Constant γ
Qi Jin  |  2026  |  Zenodo  |  DOI  |  Preprint
Qi Jin introduced in his paper a method for proving the irrationality of numbers. Building upon this approach, we present an optimized version of the method and use it to prove that the Euler–Mascheroni constant γ is transcendental, and hence irrational. Furthermore, we establish the transcendence of πe, π/e, π+e, and π−e. γ, πe, π/e, π+e, and π−e are both irrational and transcendental. In this paper, π denotes the Wallis product for π and γ denotes the Maclaurin series for γ. Finally, it is proved that all ζ(k) are transcendental.
Proof of the Algebraic Independence of π with e and γ
Qi Jin  |  2026  |  Zenodo  |  DOI  |  Preprint
This paper establishes a criterion for the algebraic independence of two numbers. Applying this theorem, we prove that π and the Euler–Mascheroni constant γ are algebraically independent. Consequently, it follows that πγ, π/γ, π+γ, and π−γ are all transcendental. Furthermore, we prove that π and e are algebraically independent. Finally, it is proved that ζ(k), π, and γ are algebraically independent. In this paper, π denotes the Wallis product for π and γ denotes the Maclaurin series for γ.
Infinite Repeating Decimals Are Transcendental and Irrational
Qi Jin  |  2026  |  Zenodo  |  DOI  |  Preprint
Based on the transcendence criterion proved by Qi Jin, it can be shown that all infinite repeating decimals are transcendental numbers, and hence irrational. Consequently, infinite repeating decimals are not rational numbers. Treating repeating decimals as rational numbers is a logical error. The infinite repeating decimal 0.(9) and the number 1 are two distinct numbers, even though the difference between them tends to zero.
